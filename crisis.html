Briefing 02Crisis deep diveSpirit Airlines · Feb – Sep 2026Proof of concept · public data

The shutdown was the headline. The damage came after.

Spirit Airlines stopped flying on 2 May 2026 after rescue talks failed. This briefing asks three questions a client would ask: could we have seen it coming, what should we do in the first 48 hours, and where does the real reputational damage happen?

What we found

70% of aftermath coverage was negative, against 27% on the shutdown days themselves.

Shutdown day brought about 700× normal coverage, and attention halved within two days. The weeks that followed were about stranded travellers, repossessed planes and unpaid staff. Our risk index went to Alert 13 days before the shutdown.

What it means

Crisis plans tend to be written for announcement day. Here, the announcement was mostly reported as fact; the lasting impression formed in the aftermath, when the company was least able to respond.

Local TV and radio carried nearly half the story. National outlets carried 4% of it.

What we recommend

Prepare on the Alert, not the announcement. Thirteen days was enough to ready customer, employee and local-media plans.

Plan the aftermath first: refunds, rebooking and staff pay are the stories that turn coverage negative.

Rivals: 13–14% of Spirit stories also named Frontier or Southwest. Be ready to be the dependable alternative.

Could we have seen it coming?

Exhibit 2.1

The risk index went to Alert 13 days before Spirit stopped flying

Spirit's weekly Reputation Risk Index, January to the shutdown, with thresholds set on the other four brands.

GDELT GKG via BigQuery. Thresholds: 90th (Watch) and 97.5th (Alert) percentiles of weekly scores for Frontier, Southwest, Cava and Dutch Bros. Spirit was not used to set them.

Analyst note · an honest backtest

What it shows: a simple, rules-based index would have put Spirit at the top of a weekly watch-list from the week ending 19 April, ahead of the 2 May shutdown.

The 1 March Alert was real but early. Coverage jumped to 12× normal, almost all about bankruptcy, even though the news was a plan to exit it.

What it doesn't show: in the two weeks before the final Alert, Spirit coverage went almost silent (3–4 articles a week), and the index read "normal". Quiet is not the same as safe, which is why an analyst reviews the watch-list too.
